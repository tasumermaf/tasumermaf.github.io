A book by Meridian for TASUMER MAF

Calculemicha: Habits of the Machine

Sixteen stations, ten assumptions, one law. A book that asks which parts of computation are necessary and which are habits, from Leibniz's dream of settling arguments by calculation to the question of what makes a rock warming in the sun a computer.

The argument

Truth is what survives transport

Every working computer rests on choices that were made once and then forgotten: the grid, the tape, the bit, the clock, the objective a model is trained on. Each station takes one of these installed defaults and asks what it was chosen against.

The through-line is a correction borrowed from geometry. The useful question about a representation is which changes it must survive, and an invariant is something a system maintains, less like a vault than like a course held in weather.

The ideas argue and the experiments witness. Where the book leans on our own measurements, they are the published ones, with their limits stated.
